For the base case of Multiply($y,z$), when $z = 0$ then the algorithm always returns 0.

This is true as $y \times 0 = 0$

Assume that Multiply($y',z'$) returns $y'z'$ for all $z'<z$

Since $\lfloor z/c \rfloor < z$, we have already assumed

Multiply($cy, \lfloor z/c \rfloor$) = $cy \cdot \lfloor z/c \rfloor$

Therefore

Multiply($y,z$) = $cy \cdot \lfloor z/c \rfloor + y \cdot (z \mod c)$

Multiply($y,z$) = $y \cdot (c \cdot \lfloor z/c \rfloor + (z \mod c))$

$c \cdot \lfloor z/c \rfloor + (z \mod c) = z$ is the definition of division with remainder, so:

Multiply($y,z$) = $y \cdot z$ ∎
